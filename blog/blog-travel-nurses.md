# House Hacking With Travel Nurses

Of all the mid-term rental audiences, traveling healthcare workers are the most consistent and the easiest to plan around. If your space fits and your location works, this is one of the strongest house hack strategies available.

## Why this audience works so well

**They need furnished housing by definition.** They're on assignment for a set number of weeks, they're not bringing furniture, and they need somewhere real to live rather than a hotel.

**Assignments have end dates.** You know months in advance when someone's leaving. That's better visibility than a standard lease, where you get thirty days' notice.

**They're rarely home.** Long shifts, often nights. Someone working three twelve-hour shifts is at your property far less than a typical resident, and they're not looking to socialize in your kitchen.

**They're generally low-drama residents.** Employed, on contract, focused on work, and leaving on a known date.

**Assignments often extend.** A thirteen-week stay that becomes twenty-six is common, and extensions are free money — no turnover, no vacancy, no marketing.

## What they actually want

The list is short and specific:

**Privacy.** Own room at minimum, own bathroom strongly preferred, own entrance ideally. Someone coming off a night shift needs to be able to sleep and decompress without navigating a household.

**Proximity to the hospital.** This is the single biggest factor. Commute matters more than neighborhood prestige. If you're not reasonably close to a major medical facility, this audience is harder to reach.

**Everything actually there.** Not just furniture — cookware, dishes, towels, sheets, a real bed, and reliable internet. The whole value proposition is arriving with a suitcase.

**Quiet, especially during the day.** Night shift workers sleep when everyone else is awake. This is worth saying out loud in your listing and worth thinking about in your house rules.

**Flexible terms.** Assignments are typically measured in weeks, and start dates shift. Rigid twelve-month thinking doesn't fit.

**Parking.** They're driving to shifts at odd hours.

## Does your space fit?

Honest checklist:

☐ Private bedroom, ideally private bathroom
☐ Reasonable commute to a major hospital or medical campus
☐ Furnished, or you have capital to furnish it
☐ Quiet enough for daytime sleep
☐ Dedicated parking
☐ Reliable internet
☐ Washer and dryer access

If you're missing privacy or proximity, this audience is a stretch. Everything else you can add.

## How to reach them

**Furnished Finder** is the platform most associated with this audience and the obvious first listing.

**Facebook groups** for travel nurses in your metro area — these exist and they're active. Read the rules before posting.

**Direct outreach to housing coordinators** at nearby facilities, if you're comfortable with that.

**Word of mouth.** This is the underrated one. Travel healthcare workers talk to each other constantly and they recommend places that treated them well. A good experience with one resident can fill your space repeatedly without you marketing at all.

## Practical things that matter

**Be flexible on start dates.** Assignments shift. A few days of flexibility makes you much easier to book.

**Price monthly, all-inclusive.** Utilities, internet, everything. Simplicity is a selling point for someone managing a temporary move.

**Have a clear, simple lease** designed for the term length. Standard year-lease paperwork doesn't fit and creates friction.

**Respond fast to inquiries.** People on assignment are often booking under time pressure, and the first responsive host usually wins.

**Ask about extensions early.** A month before their end date, ask whether they're extending. It costs you nothing and it's the cheapest occupancy you'll ever get.

## The honest caveats

**Demand is location-dependent.** This works beautifully near hospitals and not at all in areas without that demand. Before you furnish anything, find out what similar furnished spaces near you are getting and how quickly they fill.

**It's seasonal and variable.** Contract volume moves with hospital staffing needs, which fluctuate. Don't underwrite to full occupancy.

**Furnishing is real capital.** And you'll replace things. Budget for both.

**More turnovers than a year lease.** Manageable, but it's not passive.

## The short version

If you have a private furnished space near a major medical facility, traveling healthcare workers are one of the best resident profiles available to a house hacker — predictable, professional, rarely home, and frequently extending.

Check the demand near your specific address before you commit capital to furnishing. Then make the space genuinely complete, be flexible on timing, and respond fast.
