# What Your Homeowners Policy Doesn't Cover When You Rent a Room

Here's a gap most house hackers don't find out about until they file a claim: the moment you start renting out part of your home, your standard homeowners policy may not cover you the way you assume.

This is a five-minute phone call and it's the single easiest thing on the entire house hacking checklist to skip.

> **Not insurance advice.** I'm a real estate agent, not an agent of the insurance variety. Policies, definitions, and available products vary significantly by carrier and state. This is what to *ask*, not what your answer will be.

## Why it changes

A homeowners policy is generally written around the assumption that the home is occupied by you and your household. Introducing a paying resident changes the risk profile in several ways, and how your specific policy handles that varies.

The common areas worth asking about:

**Liability for someone who isn't a household member.** If a resident or their guest is injured on the property, how is that handled?

**Their belongings.** Your policy covers your property. A resident's belongings generally are not your policy's concern — which is a reason to require renters insurance from them.

**The business-use question.** Some carriers treat renting out part of a home as a business activity. Whether that affects coverage, and how, depends on the policy.

**Loss of rental income.** If the property becomes uninhabitable after a covered event, is the rent you'd have collected covered? Often not, without something specific.

**Occupancy definitions.** Some policies have language about who's living in the home and what happens when that changes.

## The conversation to have

Call your agent and say plainly what you're planning. Not "hypothetically" — actually.

Then ask:

- Does renting part of my home affect my current coverage?
- Am I covered for liability involving a resident or their guest?
- Is there a difference between renting a bedroom and renting a separate lower-level unit?
- Does it change if the space is furnished?
- Does it change if I'm doing short-term or mid-term rentals?
- Do I need an endorsement, a different policy type, or additional liability coverage?
- What happens to my coverage when I move out and rent the whole property?
- Should I require renters insurance from residents, and what should I require?

That last question is a good one to act on regardless.

## Require renters insurance

Whatever your own policy says, **require your residents to carry renters insurance and name it in the lease.**

It's inexpensive, it covers their belongings so they're not looking at you when something happens to their laptop, and it typically includes liability coverage on their side.

Ask for proof before move-in and treat it like any other move-in requirement. Most residents are fine with it; some have never thought about it and will thank you.

## The transition when you move out

This is the second insurance conversation, and it's the one people forget entirely.

When you stop living there and the whole property becomes a rental, your homeowners policy is likely no longer the right product. That's typically a different type of policy, and continuing on the wrong one is how people discover a coverage problem at the worst possible moment.

**Call before you move**, not after.

## Short-term rentals are a separate conversation

If any part of your plan involves nightly stays, that's a distinct question with distinct products. Standard policies frequently exclude it, and some platforms provide protections that are narrower than people assume.

Ask specifically about it rather than assuming it falls under the same umbrella.

## Where it fits in the sequence

Make the insurance call **before you list the space**, not after someone moves in.

It's the first item on my thirty-day checklist for a reason: it costs nothing, it takes five minutes, and the wrong time to learn the answer is after something has already happened.

## The short version

Renting out part of your home may change what your policy covers. Ask your agent directly, before anyone moves in. Require renters insurance from your residents. Call again before you move out.

Five minutes, twice, over the whole life of the property.
