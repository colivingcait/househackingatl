# How to Write a Listing That Fills a Room Fast

An empty room costs you a full month of rent. A bad listing costs you two.

The gap between a room that fills in four days and one that sits for five weeks usually isn't the space or the price. It's the listing — and specifically, whether it answers the questions people have before they have to ask.

## Photos do most of the work

More than the copy. More than the price. If you fix one thing, fix this.

**Shoot in daylight, with the lights on too.** Both. Natural light makes the room feel real; the fixtures fill the shadows.

**Open every blind.**

**Clear the floor completely.** Nothing on it. Floor space reads as room size, and clutter reads as small.

**Shoot from the corner.** Standing in a doorway and pointing straight ahead makes every room look like a closet. From the corner you capture two walls and the space looks its actual size.

**Photograph everything they'd want to see:** the room, the closet, the bathroom, the kitchen, the shared living space, the entrance, and the parking. A listing with four photos of one room and nothing else makes people assume you're hiding the rest.

**Retake them after every turnover.** Photos age. Furniture moves, paint changes. Fifteen minutes per turnover, and it's the highest-leverage thing you do.

## Say the specific things

Vague listings generate volume, not quality. You'll get twenty messages asking things the listing should have answered, and you'll spend a week replying to people who were never a fit.

Be direct about:

- **Which bathroom.** Private, shared with one person, shared with three
- **What's included.** Utilities, internet, cleaning, supplies — name them
- **The kitchen.** Shared, assigned shelves, kitchenette, full second kitchen
- **Laundry.** In unit, in the house, on site, none
- **Parking.** How many spots, assigned or not, street or driveway
- **Furnished or not.** And if furnished, what's actually there
- **Who else lives there.** Number of people, general vibe — not names
- **House expectations.** Guests, quiet hours, pets, smoking

Every specific detail filters out someone who wasn't going to work and pulls in someone who was.

## The headline

You get one line in most feeds. Use it for the two things people filter on: **what it is and where it is.**

> *Private basement suite with separate entrance — [area], utilities included*

> *Furnished room, private bath — [area], month-to-month available*

> *Room in quiet 4BR house — [area], near [major landmark or corridor]*

Skip the adjectives. "Beautiful," "amazing," and "must see" are what every listing says, which means they say nothing. A concrete feature does more work than any superlative.

## Write the first paragraph for the person you want

You're not writing an ad. You're describing a home to a specific kind of person.

> *Private lower-level suite with its own entrance, full bath, and kitchenette. Quiet house, two other residents who both work normal hours. Off-street parking, laundry on site, all utilities and internet included. Available [month].*

Four sentences, every important question answered, no fluff. Someone reading that either knows it's for them or knows it isn't — and both outcomes save you time.

## What makes a listing sit

**Too few photos.** People assume the worst about what you didn't show.

**Dark photos.** Reads as a basement in the bad sense.

**No price.** Filters you out of every search and signals you're not sure.

**Only describing the room.** People are renting a life, not a rectangle. Show them the kitchen and the entrance.

**Overpricing by a little.** This is the quiet one. Ten percent over market doesn't get you fewer inquiries — it gets you almost none, because you fall out of the search filters people actually use.

## Post in more than one place

Different platforms reach different people, and no single one has everyone.

For rooms and shared housing, Facebook Marketplace and SpareRoom are where the local activity is. For whole units, Zillow. For furnished mid-term, Furnished Finder. Post to all the ones that fit your product — it costs you nothing but the copy-paste.

## Then screen tight

A good listing generates more inquiries, and more inquiries is not the same as a good outcome.

Run your process on everyone: application, income verification, background and rental history, a real conversation, a written lease. The listing's job is to fill the top of the funnel. Your process is what decides who actually moves in.

Cast wide, screen tight. An empty room costs you one month. The wrong person costs you months.
