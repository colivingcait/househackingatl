# House Hacking vs. Buying a Rental Property

Same goal — own income-producing real estate. Two very different paths to get there, and for a first-time buyer they're not close.

## The financing gap

This is the whole comparison, honestly.

**House hack:** you're buying a home you'll live in. Owner-occupant terms — a much smaller down payment, a better interest rate, easier qualification.

**Investment property:** you're a business decision that can be unwound. Larger down payment, higher rate, stricter qualification, and typically reserve requirements on top.

On the same house, in the same neighborhood, on the same day, those are meaningfully different deals. The difference isn't marginal — it's frequently the difference between being able to buy and not.

**Winner: house hack**, decisively, for anyone who isn't already sitting on substantial capital.

## Cash required

**House hack:** less down, which means you can start years earlier.

**Investment property:** more down, plus reserves, plus you're still paying your own rent the entire time.

That last part gets missed. When you buy a rental, you keep paying to live somewhere. When you house hack, your housing cost drops immediately — so you're improving your monthly position rather than adding to your obligations.

**Winner: house hack.**

## Privacy and lifestyle

**House hack:** you're sharing your home, or at least your property. That's a real cost and it's the honest downside.

**Investment property:** you live wherever you want and the tenants are somewhere else.

**Winner: investment property**, clearly. This is the trade you're making.

## Learning curve

**House hack:** you're on site. You see problems as they happen, you learn what breaks and how residents actually use a space, and you're there to let the plumber in.

**Investment property:** you're learning at a distance, often with a manager between you and the reality.

Being on site during your first year is genuinely educational in a way that's hard to replicate. You find out whether you like this business while it's cheap to find out.

**Winner: house hack.**

## Risk

**House hack:** one property, one loan, and you live there — so worst case, you have a place to live and a housing cost you can absorb. Vacancy hurts but doesn't leave you paying two housing costs.

**Investment property:** vacancy means you're paying your own housing plus a mortgage with no offsetting income. That's a harder position.

**Winner: house hack.**

## Scale and speed

**House hack:** one at a time, and each one requires you to actually move in. Slower.

**Investment property:** you can buy several without changing where you live.

**Winner: investment property**, if you have the capital. Which is the whole condition.

## Where the investment property actually wins

I don't want to make this one-sided. Buy the rental instead if:

- You have substantial capital and the financing gap doesn't constrain you
- You genuinely don't want to share your home, and no amount of separation changes that
- Your living situation is fixed — you own a home you're not leaving, or you can't move
- You want to buy multiple properties faster than a move-in cycle allows

Those are legitimate reasons and they're not rare.

## The answer for most first-timers

**Do both — sequentially.**

House hack first. Use the owner-occupant financing while it's available to you. Learn the business while you're living in it. Let your housing cost drop and route the difference into savings.

Then, when you move out, the house hack *becomes* the investment property. You now own a rental you bought on terms an investor could never have gotten, and you have the savings and the experience to buy the next one.

That's not a compromise between the two options. It's the path that gets you both, in the order that makes each one easier.

## The one-line version

If you have the capital and don't want to share, buy the rental. If you're starting out, the house hack is a better deal on every dimension except privacy — and it turns into the rental anyway.
